Tense & Voice

Tense isn't 12 formulas to memorize — it's a position in a 2D space: time on one axis, viewpoint on the other. Voice is a separate switch entirely: which side of the action gets the spotlight.

Foundational · used across Daily, Workplace & Academic English alike

I. Tense as a Coordinate System

Put time on the X-axis (past / present / future) and viewpoint on the Y-axis (simple / progressive / perfect). Every tense is just one coordinate on this grid.

Present + Perfect (highlighted) is the one coordinate most learners struggle with — it links a past action to a present result, a concept many languages don't grammaticalize the same way.

II. Voice — Which Side Gets the Spotlight

Active and passive voice describe the exact same event. The only thing that changes is which side of the action — the doer or the receiver — is in focus.

Active — spotlight on the source

●

The server processed the request.

The energy source (the server) leads the sentence and acts outward onto the request.

Passive — spotlight on the receiver

●●
be + V-pp

The request was processed by the server.

The receiver (the request) leads instead, and the source is pushed to the background — or dropped entirely: The request was processed.

Practice

Plot this sentence on the grid: "By next year, she will have shipped four products." Which coordinate is it, and why?

Show answer

Future + Perfect. "By next year" sets a future reference point, and "will have shipped" describes an action that will already be complete by that point — the defining move of the Perfect row: a bridge connecting two moments in time, not just a single point.
