The Sound Map — 44 Building Blocks of American English

Every English word is built from just 44 distinct sounds. Learn to hear and shape each one by understanding how airflow moves through your mouth — click any card to hear it spoken.

Foundational · used across Daily, Workplace & Academic English alike

Intuition

Every sound a human voice makes comes down to one physical question: how much does the airflow get blocked on its way out?

Vowels are sounds where air flows out freely — only the shape of your lips and tongue changes the sound. Consonants are sounds where the airflow is blocked or squeezed somewhere in the mouth or throat. That single idea explains all 44 sounds below.

Practice

ship and chip differ by exactly one sound. Which consonant sound changes, and what is the key difference in how it's produced?

Show answer

ship uses /ʃ/, a fricative — air is squeezed through a narrow gap with continuous friction. chip uses /tʃ/, an affricate — the airflow is fully stopped first, then released with friction. Say them back to back and feel the difference: /ʃ/ can be held ("shhh"), while /tʃ/ can't.
