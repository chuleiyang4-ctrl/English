Sentence Patterns & the Roles Words Play

Stop memorizing grammar rules one at a time. Picture a sentence as a small structure under load: parts of speech are the materials, and every sentence — no matter how long — reduces to one of 5 load-bearing patterns.

Foundational · used across Daily, Workplace & Academic English alike

I. What Each Part of Speech Actually Does

The 8 parts of speech aren't an arbitrary list to memorize — each one plays a specific structural role in a sentence.

Noun
🏗️ the material

The load-bearing piece — subject or object. apple, idea, system.

Verb
⚡ the engine

Drives the whole sentence forward. No verb, no motion. run, create, is.

Adjective
🎨 the surface

Attaches to a noun and changes how it appears. blue, quantum, fast.

Preposition
🔗 the joint

Connects space, time, and logic between pieces. in, on, under, through.

II. The 5 Load-Bearing Sentence Patterns

However long or twisted a sentence gets, strip away the decoration and one of these 5 skeletons is always underneath.

Solid arrows = energy transfer. Dashed lines = an "equals sign," not a transfer — the linking verb (V) or the object complement (OC) just describes, it doesn't hand anything over.

1. Subject + Verb — a self-contained engine

●
S + V

All the energy is spent inside the subject — nothing is released outward onto anything else.

The sun shines.

2. Subject + Verb + Object — energy transfers one-way

●
S + V + O

The verb carries energy directly from the subject onto a receiving object.

Developers build platforms.

3. Subject + Linking Verb + Complement — a state equation

●●
S + V + C

A linking verb doesn't transfer energy at all — it acts as an "equals sign" between the subject and its state.

Knowledge is power.

4. Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object — a double handoff

●●
S + V + IO + DO

The action carries both a receiver and a thing being transferred to that receiver.

AI gives us clarity.

5. Subject + Verb + Object + Object Complement — description added after

●●●
S + V + O + OC

The object alone doesn't finish the thought — a complement must be added describing its resulting state.

Code makes systems efficient.

Practice

Identify the sentence pattern: "The team elected her captain."

Show answer

S + V + O + OC. "The team" (S) "elected" (V) "her" (O) "captain" (OC) — "her" received the action, and "captain" describes what she became as a result. Without "captain," the sentence would feel incomplete: you'd naturally ask "elected her what?"
